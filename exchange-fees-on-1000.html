How much do exchange fees cost on 1,000?

Separate percentage charges, fixed fees, and the exchange-rate margin with a transparent worked example.

By Rate Atelier · November 26, 2026 · Editorial policy

Three costs can hide behind one conversion

The result depends on the quoted exchange rate, any percentage charge, and any fixed charge. “No commission” describes only one part of the calculation. Compare the final spending amount or recipient payout for the same total budget.

Compare travel methods if charges are added to the exchanged principal. Use Compare your quotes for a provider quote with a fixed sender fee deducted from your budget.

Example 1: a 3% fee inside a 1,000 USD budget

At a hypothetical rate of 0.90 EUR per USD, a conversion with no fees would yield 900 EUR. If a 3% fee is added to the exchanged principal, that principal must be 1,000 ÷ 1.03 = 970.873786 USD. The fee is about 29.126214 USD, leaving 873.786408 EUR, displayed as 873.79 EUR.

This is different from charging 3% on the entire 1,000 USD and deducting 30 USD. In that model, 970 USD converts to 873 EUR. The fee basis matters; ask which amount the percentage applies to.

Example 2: a fixed transfer fee

If a transfer provider deducts a 5 USD fee from the same 1,000 USD total budget and quotes 0.90 EUR per USD, the recipient gets (1,000 − 5) × 0.90 = 895.50 EUR. That is the model used by our transfer quote calculator.

Example 3: no fee, but a weaker rate

If another provider charges no explicit fee but quotes 0.88 EUR per USD, the recipient gets 880 EUR. That is 15.50 EUR less than the 5 USD fee quote above. At the assumed reference rate of 0.90, the shortfall is 20 EUR. The shortfall measures the difference from the benchmark; it is not a separate invoice charge.
