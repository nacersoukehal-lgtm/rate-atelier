Card versus cash abroad: compare the total cost

A practical way to compare card purchases, ATM withdrawals, and cash exchange without overlooking fixed charges.

By Rate Atelier · November 26, 2026 · Editorial policy

Start with one budget, not one advertised fee

A card with a small percentage fee and an ATM with a fixed charge cannot be compared from the fee labels alone. Choose a total budget in your home currency, then calculate how much local spending power remains after every charge.

Our travel exchange-cost calculator uses the same total budget for all three methods. It does not fetch card or exchange-desk offers: you enter the terms quoted to you.

A worked example with 1,000 USD

These are hypothetical inputs, not current offers. Suppose the reference rate is 0.90 EUR per USD. A fee-free conversion of 1,000 USD would be 900 EUR.

Illustrative comparison within a 1,000 USD total budget
MethodAssumptionsSpendable EUR
Card3% fee, no fixed fee, rate 0.90873.79
ATM2% fee, two charges of 5 USD, rate 0.90873.53
Cash exchangeNo percentage fee, 10 USD fixed fee, rate 0.88871.20

The card’s exchanged principal is 1,000 ÷ 1.03, or about 970.8738 USD. At 0.90 EUR per dollar, it buys about 873.7864 EUR. For the ATM, subtract 10 USD first, divide 990 by 1.02, and multiply by 0.90. The cash desk charges no percentage fee, but its weaker rate still affects the result.

Check the fee basis before comparing

This calculator assumes percentage fees are added to the exchanged principal and fixed charges are paid within your budget. If a provider deducts a percentage from the budget instead, use its actual quote in the transfer comparison tool. Include both bank and ATM-operator charges. Avoid counting the exchange-rate spread twice if it is already included in your quoted rate.

The FDIC travel guidance recommends checking ATM and foreign transaction charges with your bank before travel. Confirm those terms for your own account.

Keep a useful record

Copy a calculation link to preserve the inputs and reference rate, or download the dated summary. Recheck the terms before using the result: a reference rate is a comparison benchmark, and the actual rate or fees can change.
