How Much Does Gold Need to Rise for You to Break Even?

By Rate Atelier · November 14, 2026

If you pay $2,630 for a one-ounce gold bar and sell it to a dealer who pays 98% of spot, gold needs to reach about $2,684 per troy ounce for you to break even, assuming the buyback discount holds and selling costs are zero. If spot was $2,500 when you bought, that’s a rise of about 7.35%.

Two dealer quotes tell you more than one spot price

The spot price gives you a reference per fine troy ounce. A dealer’s selling price can include a premium for the physical bar or coin. The same dealer may quote a lower price to buy that item back. Ask for both quotes on the same product at roughly the same time. A low premium on the purchase can lose its advantage if the buyback quote is also low. The Royal Mint’s guide to buying and selling prices.

Calculate the break-even gold price

  1. Add your purchase costs. Use the checkout total, including the dealer premium, shipping, and any applicable tax.
  2. Estimate what it costs to sell. Include return shipping, insurance, or flat fees you expect to pay.
  3. Find the dealer’s buyback percentage. If spot is $2,500 and the dealer would pay $2,450 for one fine troy ounce, the quote is 98% of spot.

Break-even spot price per ounce = (total purchase cost + fixed selling costs) ÷ (fine troy ounces × buyback fraction).

The formula assumes the future buyback remains the same fraction of spot. If the dealer uses a fixed dollar discount per ounce instead, add that discount to your per-ounce break-even cost. If the buyback terms change, use the new terms and recalculate.

Worked example for a one-ounce gold bar

Say spot is $2,500, the dealer charges $2,600 for the bar, and insured delivery adds $30. The bar contains one fine troy ounce, giving you a total purchase cost of $2,630. With a buyback at 98% of spot, divide $2,630 by 0.98 to get $2,683.67. The figures are illustrative.

  • If gold rises 5%: spot reaches $2,625. At 98% of spot, you receive $2,572.50, which leaves you $57.50 short.
  • If return shipping costs $25: divide $2,655 by 0.98. Break-even moves to $2,709.18, about 8.37% above your original spot price.

What to check before comparing bars or coins

Use the fine gold content listed for the product. Some coins contain alloy, so their total weight can differ from their fine gold weight. Compare dealer quotes in the same currency and check whether the buyback is set when you place the order or when the dealer receives the metal. If you pay in a different currency from the one you’ll receive when selling, exchange rates and conversion fees can shift your break-even price in your home currency.
