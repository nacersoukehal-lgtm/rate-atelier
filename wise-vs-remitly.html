Why a lower fee can still give a lower payout

For a fixed budget, a weaker exchange rate can outweigh a fee saving. If the transfer fee comes out of that budget, the calculation is:

Recipient payout = (total budget − transfer fee) × quoted exchange rate.

The example below uses invented quotes, not current Wise or Remitly prices. Both assume a $500 total budget with the fee deducted before conversion.

Quote A delivers EUR 2.28 more, despite its higher fee. If a provider adds fees on top of the amount sent, adjust the transfer amount so both quotes use the same total budget. Include known third-party charges separately when they are not part of the quoted payout.

A practical checklist for your next transfer

  1. Choose a total budget and the same receiving currency.
  2. Get both quotes close together using the payment method you will actually use.
  3. Check the recipient’s delivery option and estimated arrival time.
  4. Record fees, the quoted exchange rate and final payout; check promotion eligibility.
  5. Enter your quotes in the comparison tool, then verify the final checkout amounts with the provider.

Is Wise or Remitly better for repeat transfers?

Compare a normal repeat-transfer quote as well as any welcome offer. A one-off discount does not establish the cost of sending every month. Keep a note of the quote date and method so later comparisons use the same basis.

Does “no transfer fee” mean no exchange cost?

No. A transfer with a zero stated fee can still deliver less because of its exchange rate. Our historical reference-rate lookup provides context, but a reference rate is not an executable provider quote or a promise of the payout you will receive.
