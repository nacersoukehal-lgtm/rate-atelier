What percentage of melt value is this gold offer?

Compare a dealer’s net offer with an estimate of the metal content, using weight, purity, and a dated reference price.

By Rate Atelier · November 26, 2026 · Editorial policy

Compare the net payout with the metal estimate

First estimate the value of the gold content. Then subtract the dealer’s deductions from the offer. Divide that net payout by the estimated melt value and multiply by 100. The result answers one specific question: what share of the reference metal value would you receive?

Open the melt calculator and dealer comparison to enter both offers alongside the same metal estimate.

Worked example: a 10-gram 18K item

Assume the item weighs 10 grams after excluding stones and other non-metal parts. At 18K, the gold fraction is 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75, so the estimated pure-gold content is 7.5 grams.

For this example only, assume pure gold is worth 100 USD per gram. This is an illustrative reference, not today’s market quote. The estimated melt value is 7.5 × 100 = 750 USD.

Two hypothetical offers for the same item
DealerOfferDeductionsNet payoutShare of melt
A600 USD15 USD585 USD78.00%
B640 USD25 USD615 USD82.00%

Dealer A’s percentage is (600 − 15) ÷ 750 × 100 = 78%. Dealer B pays 30 USD more after deductions, with a net payout equal to 82% of the estimated metal value.

A percentage is a comparison, not a valuation guarantee

Weight and purity must match the item, and the reference quote must use the correct unit. Our calculator uses USD per troy ounce and converts from the selected weight unit. Bullion products have different weights and fineness specifications; the U.S. Mint’s bullion information illustrates why the exact product matters.

The estimate values metal content. It does not price craftsmanship, collectibility, stones, or resale demand, and it does not promise a particular dealer payout. Ask for all deductions and compare net offers at approximately the same reference time.

Save the basis of your comparison

The calculator can create a result link with weight, purity, reference price, and offers. Its downloadable summary records the source and calculation time. Keep that reference date with your result so an old estimate is not mistaken for a new quote.
